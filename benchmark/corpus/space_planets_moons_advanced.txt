Space, Planets, and Moons – Advanced Knowledge Base

The Solar System

The Solar System formed approximately 4.57 billion years ago from the
gravitational collapse of a giant molecular cloud. More than 99.8% of
the Solar System’s mass is contained in the Sun. The remaining mass is
distributed among planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets,
meteoroids, and interplanetary dust.

The eight planets, in order from the Sun, are Mercury, Venus, Earth,
Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has
virtually no substantial atmosphere, only a thin exosphere composed
mainly of oxygen, sodium, hydrogen, helium, and potassium. Mercury
completes one orbit around the Sun in approximately 88 Earth days and
rotates three times for every two orbits due to its 3:2 spin-orbit
resonance. Daytime temperatures can exceed 430°C while nighttime
temperatures fall below −180°C.

Venus

Venus is similar in size to Earth but has an atmosphere composed of
about 96.5% carbon dioxide with clouds of sulfuric acid. Surface
pressure is about 92 times that of Earth. Venus rotates in a retrograde
direction, meaning it spins opposite to most planets. Its surface
temperature averages around 465°C due to an extreme runaway greenhouse
effect.

Earth

Earth is currently the only known planet to support life. About 71% of
its surface is covered by liquid water. Earth’s atmosphere is composed
primarily of nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%). The Moon stabilizes
Earth’s axial tilt, helping maintain a relatively stable climate over
geological time.

Earth’s Moon

Earth’s Moon is the fifth-largest natural satellite in the Solar System.
It is tidally locked, always showing the same face to Earth. The Moon
causes ocean tides and is gradually moving away from Earth at
approximately 3.8 cm per year.

Mars

Mars is known as the Red Planet because of iron oxide on its surface.
Olympus Mons is the tallest volcano in the Solar System, rising about 22
km above the surrounding plains. Valles Marineris is a canyon system
stretching over 4,000 km. Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos.
Phobos is slowly spiraling toward Mars and may eventually break apart to
form a ring.

Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System and is primarily
composed of hydrogen and helium. The Great Red Spot is a giant storm
that has persisted for centuries. Jupiter has a powerful magnetic field
approximately 20,000 times stronger than Earth’s. It has more than 90
confirmed moons.

Major Jovian Moons

Io is the most volcanically active body in the Solar System due to tidal
heating. Europa possesses a global subsurface ocean beneath an icy crust
and is considered one of the best places to search for extraterrestrial
microbial life. Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System and is
larger than Mercury. It has its own intrinsic magnetic field. Callisto
is heavily cratered and likely contains a buried salty ocean.

Saturn

Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system composed primarily of
water ice particles. Saturn has the lowest average density of all the
planets and would theoretically float in a sufficiently large body of
water.

Titan

Titan is Saturn’s largest moon and possesses a dense nitrogen-rich
atmosphere. Lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane exist near its
poles. Titan has an active methane cycle analogous to Earth’s water
cycle.

Enceladus

Enceladus ejects water-rich plumes from fractures near its south pole.
These plumes contain water vapor, salts, silica particles, methane,
carbon dioxide, and organic molecules, indicating a global subsurface
ocean.

Uranus

Uranus rotates on its side with an axial tilt of about 98 degrees. This
unusual orientation likely resulted from a massive collision early in
its history. Uranus appears blue-green because methane absorbs red
wavelengths of sunlight.

Neptune

Neptune is the most distant major planet. It experiences the fastest
winds in the Solar System, exceeding 2,000 km/h. Triton, its largest
moon, orbits in a retrograde direction and is believed to be a captured
Kuiper Belt object. Triton has active nitrogen geysers.

Dwarf Planets

Pluto is located in the Kuiper Belt and has five known moons. Charon is
so large relative to Pluto that they orbit a common barycenter outside
Pluto itself. Other recognized dwarf planets include Eris, Haumea,
Makemake, and Ceres.

Asteroids

Most asteroids orbit within the main asteroid belt between Mars and
Jupiter. Ceres is both the largest asteroid belt object and a dwarf
planet. Asteroids preserve clues about the early Solar System.

Comets

Comets originate mainly from the Kuiper Belt and the distant Oort Cloud.
As a comet approaches the Sun, solar radiation causes volatile ices to
sublimate, producing a glowing coma and one or more tails. The ion tail
always points away from the Sun because of the solar wind.

Exoplanets

An exoplanet is a planet orbiting a star other than the Sun. Thousands
have been discovered using methods such as the transit method, radial
velocity measurements, gravitational microlensing, and direct imaging.

Black Holes

A black hole forms when a sufficiently massive star collapses under
gravity after exhausting its nuclear fuel. The event horizon marks the
boundary beyond which nothing, not even light, can escape. Supermassive
black holes exist at the centers of most large galaxies.

Neutron Stars

Neutron stars are extremely dense stellar remnants formed after certain
supernova explosions. A teaspoon of neutron-star material would weigh
billions of tons on Earth. Pulsars are rapidly rotating neutron stars
that emit beams of electromagnetic radiation.

Galaxies

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy containing hundreds of billions
of stars. Our Solar System is located in the Orion Arm approximately
26,000 light-years from the galactic center, where the supermassive
black hole Sagittarius A* resides.

Cosmology

The observable universe is estimated to be about 13.8 billion years old.
According to the Big Bang model, the universe has been expanding since
its origin. Dark matter accounts for roughly 27% of the universe’s
energy content, while dark energy accounts for about 68%. Ordinary
matter makes up only about 5%.

Interesting Facts

-   Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth.
-   One light-year equals approximately 9.46 trillion kilometers.
-   Jupiter’s magnetosphere is the largest planetary structure in the
    Solar System.
-   Venus has a longer day than its year.
-   Ganymede is larger than the planet Mercury.
-   Titan is the only moon with a dense atmosphere.
-   Europa and Enceladus are leading candidates in the search for
    extraterrestrial microbial life.
-   The James Webb Space Telescope studies the early universe primarily
    in infrared wavelengths.
